Supplement · Figures 1–13

The Ms of Geriatrics

From four to N, one M at a time.

Background Older adults are complex. The supply of clinically relevant words beginning with M is finite but, it turns out, larger than five.

Methods Beginning from the 4Ms of an Age-Friendly Health System (Figure 1) and the Geriatric 5Ms (Figure 2), one M was added per figure. Each figure introduces only its new M; the earlier Ms are assumed, as in practice.

Results Fifteen Ms, then all of them. Figures 1 and 2 are real. Figures 3 through 13 are not, although Figure 7 has a point.

Conclusions See Figure 13. See all of them.

Figure 1 · Canonical

The 4Ms

Age-Friendly Health Systems. The John A. Hartford Foundation, the Institute for Healthcare Improvement, and the American Hospital Association.

What Matters
Know and align care with each older adult's specific health outcome goals and care preferences, including day-to-day needs as well as end-of-life planning, across settings of care.
Medication
If medication is necessary, use age-friendly medications that do not interfere with What Matters to the older adult, Mobility, or Mentation across settings of care.
Mentation
Prevent, identify, treat, and manage dementia, depression, and delirium across settings of care.
Mobility
Ensure that older adults move safely every day in order to maintain function and do What Matters.

Figure 2 · Canonical

The 5Ms

Tinetti M, Huang A, Molnar F. J Am Geriatr Soc. 2017;65(9):2115. Icons after the Stanford and VA Boston teaching slides.

Mind
Mentation, dementia, delirium, depression.
Mobility
Impaired gait and balance; fall injury prevention.
Medications
Polypharmacy, deprescribing, optimal prescribing; adverse effects and medication burden.
Multicomplexity
Multimorbidity; complex bio-psycho-social situations.
Matters Most
Each individual's own meaningful health outcome goals and care preferences.
